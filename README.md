# Text Flows — Workshop Materials

> Text is not just a medium — it's the **interface** for cognition with LLMs.

Effective work with large language models depends on a skill almost nobody
teaches: **moving text well** — extracting, transforming, splitting,
recombining, and versioning the words that flow between human and machine.
Not prompt engineering: the layer *under* it.

This repository holds the complete, reusable materials for **Text Flows**,
a workshop that teaches these skills to university staff and other
professionals who work with AI daily but have never been shown that their
copy-paste labor is a learnable, improvable craft.

## Provenance

- The full workshop was delivered once to date: **July 1, 2025**, to
  university engineering staff — **61 participants** joined the session's
  live-interaction platform (the primary attendance record; a handful of
  attendees may not have connected).
- A **30-minute conference-talk variant** of the material was presented
  **May 5, 2025** at a university AI summit; the deck here descends from
  both deliveries.
- The collaborative activity (the *LLM Use-Cases Documentation Sprint*)
  was piloted earlier with ~80 students in an undergraduate software
  engineering course before being adapted for staff.

All participant-facing evidence in `results/` is aggregate-only; the FAQ
attributes questions to role-anonymous participant labels.

**Provenance boundary** (which files are the 2025 delivery's, and which
are new): the deck, worksheet, FAQ, and facilitation guide in
`workshop/` are the delivery's own artifacts, de-branded and
de-identified. The `exercises/`, `guides/`, and `workflows/` directories
were **authored for this release (September 2026)** as the reusable
guided-practice layer the delivery improvised live — a menu for
adopters, not a record of what ran.

## The idea, in one paragraph

Most AI-literacy training teaches *prompting*. But watch anyone work with
an LLM for an hour and you'll see the real bottleneck: text arriving with
broken formatting, context assembled by hand from six windows, transcripts
trapped in players, conversations that can't be moved between tools. The
workshop names this invisible layer — **text flows** — and teaches eleven
concrete tools and practices for it, from clipboard managers and format
cleaners to speech-to-text, transcript mining, and terminal one-liners.
The goal is captured by the workshop's own maxim: use LLMs to **displace**
effort, never to **replace** your intentionality — and master the text
layer so the displacement actually happens.
