# Facilitation Guide — the LLM Use-Cases Documentation Sprint

*The workshop's collaborative activity: from individual discoveries to
one shared, sorted spreadsheet. Runs ~80 minutes; scales from one table
to a full room. Piloted with ~80 students in an undergraduate software
engineering course, then adapted for university staff.*

## Why this activity

- **Nobody knows your job better than you do.** The people in the room
  hold the real map of what AI is good for in this organization.
- **Any successful integration of AI in a workplace is the collective
  effort of every member.** The activity turns private experimentation
  into shared, discussable knowledge — including the feelings (guilt,
  authorship anxiety, fear of judgment) that otherwise keep it private.
- The output is a genuinely useful artifact: a clean, deduplicated,
  importance-ranked spreadsheet of real use cases with the prompts and
  data they need.

## Logistics you need

- Tables of ~5–8 people.
- One **shared spreadsheet per table** (any collaborative sheet works).
  The 8-column schema is below; a pre-made template with these columns
  saves five minutes.
- A way for note-takers to **submit their sheet link** at the end (a
  short form, or just email).
- Optional: a live-polling tool for questions during the session.
- A visible timer.

## The five steps

### 1 · Goal-Setting (5 min)

Announce the objective: build a spreadsheet of LLM use-case findings into
**one clean, sorted list**. Walk the five steps once:

1. Brainstorm individually
2. Organize table roles
3. Share in round-robin
4. Merge & clean
5. Sort by importance

Set expectations: **every voice will be heard**; you are learning *with*
your colleagues.

### 2 · Individual Brainstorming (7 min)

Each person, **silently** (this seeds the group discussion):

1. Open personal notes or a mini-sheet.
2. **List up to five use cases** you have personally tried.
3. Capture for each:
   - One-sentence purpose
   - Key prompt or data needed
   - 1-to-5 effectiveness rating
   - Quick observation ("worked only in GPT-4"…)

### 3 · Table-Team Organization (5 min)

Each table decides, right now:

- **Product Owner** — keeps the process on track
- **Note Taker** — maintains the spreadsheet (or coordinates others)
- Logistics: will members enter their own rows *live*, or hand
  mini-sheets to the note-taker?

> Copy the shared spreadsheet link and make sure everyone can edit.

### 4 · Round-Robin Sharing (~18 min)

- Go around the table; each person speaks **uninterrupted**, ~1–2 min
  each (adjust to table size).
- Focus per person: one-sentence summary · data/prompts needed ·
  effectiveness rating · notable observations.
- **Don't triage in this phase.** Everything gets logged.

### 5 · Merge, Clean, Sort (~45 min total)

**Group Merging & Consolidation (20 min)** — identify overlaps, combine
similar ideas while keeping unique twists, refine wording. Everyone keeps
the shared sheet open to watch changes live.

**Spreadsheet Cleanup (10 min)** — every row gets the eight columns:

| # | Column |
|---|---|
| 1 | One-Sentence Summary |
| 2 | Category |
| 3 | Popularity (# of people) |
| 4 | Example Prompts |
| 5 | Data Required |
| 6 | Models Tested |
| 7 | Effectiveness |
| 8 | Notes |

Fill gaps by asking table members; standardize terminology and rating
scales.

**Sorting by Importance (15 min)** — define importance first (number of
people who need it? potential impact? real-world frequency?), then rank:
High/Medium/Low or numeric 1–10, by simple majority or dot-voting. Enter
the ranking in a Priority column.

### Final step: Submit & Celebrate 🎉

Note-takers submit the wrap-up form with the spreadsheet link. Give the
room a hand — the list helps the entire organization level up.

## Bonus: the 2-Minute Mini-Drill ("Managing Up with AI")

A compact stand-alone exercise for the executive-summary flow:

**Prompt scaffold** (participants copy-paste and fill the blanks):

```
You are my briefing assistant.

Context (1-2 sentences):  <paste here>

Ask:  <what you need from the exec>

Key Points:
1. …
2. …
3. …

Risks / Mitigations:
- …

Decision Needed by:  <date>
```

**The drill** (timer on screen):

1. **Open** yesterday's longest email chain.
2. **Paste** it under the scaffold prompt.
3. **Review** the AI draft → tweak tone & accuracy.
4. **Share** in pairs: *what did the AI miss?*

Step 4 is the pedagogical payload: the AI's role is first-draft
generator; **your** role is final judgment.

## Facilitator notes from the first deliveries

- The activity reliably surfaces use cases unique to each participant —
  in the student pilot, every member of a typical 8-person group
  contributed at least one use case nobody else had. That fact, told to
  the room in advance, is good motivation.
- The round-robin's no-triage rule matters: premature filtering silences
  exactly the shy, odd, most interesting entries.
- Timing is honest at ~80 minutes; with a short lecture before it, plan
  a half-day session or trim the sorting phase.
- Close with the release ethic that animates these materials: **release
  everything in plain text**, so colleagues can start combining your
  materials into their own flows.
